# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary visitors are people exploring Mikhail's public personal laboratory: peers interested in AI and development, and readers who want to follow experiments, notes, and projects. Their job is to understand what he is building and how he thinks.

## Product Purpose

MKH_LOG is Mikhail's personal public diary and portfolio. It collects working experiments, technical notes, and GitHub projects in one place. Success means that a visitor remembers the author and can comfortably explore the material.

## Positioning

The site documents a young builder's practical AI laboratory: local models, generative tools, development, and automation are shown as an evolving hands-on practice rather than as generic expertise claims.

## Operating Context

Visitors browse a one-page web experience with an interactive terminal, project links, and an archive of readable notes. GitHub repositories are the primary external evidence for project work.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The current implementation is a Next.js/React web application.
- The site includes project links, article reading overlays, section navigation, and a command-style terminal.
- Preserve the personal brand, GitHub as evidence, and the AI laboratory as central product facts.
- Contact channels are deliberately undecided; do not add public contact details or a contact form without a separate product decision.

## Brand Commitments

- Personal brand: Mikhail, student and builder.
- Personal, direct voice; the site is a public diary rather than a corporate portfolio.
- The themes of AI, local models, automation, development, and open-source work are factual parts of the identity.

## Evidence on Hand

- Project data and GitHub links: `src/data/portfolio.ts`.
- Three authored technical and personal notes with full text: `src/data/portfolio.ts`.
- No testimonials, clients, metrics, contact channel, or commercial claim is available; future work must not fabricate them.

## Product Principles

- Show work through artifacts and readable notes, not inflated claims.
- Keep the author's evolving practice human and specific.
- Make exploratory material easy to enter, scan, and return to.
- Let the experience be memorable without obscuring the content.
